# Why Irish Construction Firms Are Losing Money to Admin (And What to Do About It)

Your site manager is on the phone at 7am chasing a subcontractor timesheet that should have arrived two days ago. Your quantity surveyor is rebuilding a cost report from three different spreadsheets. Your accounts payable clerk is manually keying supplier invoices into your accounts system. None of that is construction. All of it is costing you money.

Irish construction is booming on paper. The market is expected to grow by 6.6% this year to reach €11.83 billion. But volume does not mean margin. The firms that will come out ahead are the ones that stop paying people to shuffle data and start letting software do it instead.

---

## The Productivity Gap Nobody Talks About

Ireland has a problem that pre-dates the current building surge. Productivity in the Irish construction sector is approximately 30% below other European countries, as highlighted by the Irish Fiscal Advisory Council. That gap does not live only on the site. A large slice of it lives in the back office.

Over 14 hours per week are wasted on unproductive activities like looking for project information, resolving conflicts and managing mistakes and rework. Fourteen hours. Per person. Per week. Multiply that across a site team of ten and you are burning through 140 hours of paid time every week without a single brick being laid.

Rework compounds the problem further. Rework accounts for up to 20% of total cost and up to 30% of total time in commercial construction, according to McKinsey. The root cause in most cases is not bad tradespeople. It is bad information flow. The wrong drawing version sent to the wrong subcontractor. A variation that never made it from the contracts manager to the site foreman. A RFI that sat in someone's inbox for four days.

---

## Where the Hours Are Actually Going

Ask any operations manager at a mid-size Irish contractor where time disappears and you will hear the same answers. Timesheets. Purchase orders. Subcontractor invoices. Compliance documentation. Variation orders. Site reports.

Manual processes drain profitability directly. One company spent 30 hours every week processing timesheets, generating invoices and managing approvals. By automating those workflows they reduced this to 4 hours, reclaiming 26 hours per week for project oversight, client relationships and business development.

That is the equivalent of a full-time role recovered without hiring anyone.

Most construction companies have data scattered across email, spreadsheets, shared drives and multiple software systems. The project manager is working from a different version of the cost report than the quantity surveyor. The contracts administrator is chasing approvals by WhatsApp. The accounts payable clerk is re-entering data that already exists in the project management tool. Every handoff is a potential error. Every manual entry is a potential delay.

---

## What Automation Actually Looks Like in a Construction Business

This is not about replacing your existing tools with a single enterprise platform that costs six figures and takes a year to bed in. Construction automation does not require replacing your entire software stack. Modern solutions integrate with existing systems.

The highest-value processes to automate first are almost always the same ones:

**Timesheet and payroll data capture.** Instead of the site foreman collecting paper sheets and the payroll administrator re-keying them on a Friday afternoon, a simple mobile form feeds directly into your payroll system. The data enters once. It flows through automatically.

**Purchase order and invoice approval workflows.** The procurement coordinator raises a PO in a structured form. It routes to the right project manager for approval, then on to the accounts payable clerk for payment, with no email chains, no lost attachments and a full audit trail. Material requisition workflows, permits and approvals, and subcontractor onboarding are among the core processes that can be digitised and automated, replacing outdated manual steps.

**Compliance and safety documentation.** The health and safety of workers is at risk when relying on manual health and safety processes. Using manual methods to record risk assessments is prone to data errors. Automated checklists, digital sign-offs and timestamped records protect your firm and make audits straightforward.

**Variation order tracking.** Every variation that is undocumented or delayed is money left on the table. An automated variation log with built-in approval routing means the contracts manager, the site manager and the client are always working from the same record.

## The Business Case Is Not Complicated

Construction companies that automate core operations report 18 to 35% higher project margins and 25 to 40% lower administrative overhead.

You do not need to automate everything. Most construction companies find that their three or four most painful manual processes consume 40 to 50% of administrative time. Focus automation there first.

For a firm turning over €5 million, cutting administrative overhead by 25% is not a rounding error. It is a meaningful shift in what the business earns from the same volume of work.

According to Deloitte and Autodesk's State of Digital Adoption in the Construction 2025 report, businesses that adopt more technologies are more likely to finish projects on time. On-time delivery means fewer penalties, faster final account settlement and better relationships with main contractors and clients.
